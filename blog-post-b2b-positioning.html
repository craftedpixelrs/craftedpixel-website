Your competitors all sound the same. Here’s how to not.

Strategy January 15, 2026 CraftedPixel

Open five B2B homepages in adjacent tabs and blur the logos. You will read some variation of “streamline,” “empower teams,” and “AI-powered” whether they sell payroll, data pipelines, or catering software. Buyers notice—and they default to checklist comparisons on price alone.

Differentiation is not louder adjectives. It is a specific story about who loses if they do not pick you.

Start with the buyer, not the roadmap

We ask three questions in workshops: Who signs? Who blocks? What do they fear will go wrong in the first 90 days? The site should speak to that fear with plain language—then show proof you have shipped through it before.

Trade vague claims for concrete proof

  • Replace “world-class support” with response-time SLAs and named channels.
  • Swap generic logos for short outcome captions (role, metric, timeframe—anonymized if needed).
  • Use comparison tables only when you can cite verifiable differences—not marketing synonyms.

Let design carry tone

Positioning is not only copy. Typography, photography, and motion set expectations about whether you are enterprise-stable or builder-fast. When those signals fight the message, visitors trust neither.

You do not need a rebrand to fix 80% of this. Often a tighter hero, a credible proof band, and a pricing or security page that answers real objections will outperform a clever slogan nobody believes.

Homepage vs. campaign landers

Your homepage should answer “why you, why now” for the broadest serious visitor. Campaign pages can be sharper and weirder—they only need to convert one segment. We see teams paste the same vague hero on every URL; better to keep the brand consistent but let each lander name the scenario (e.g. SOC 2 timeline, migration from a named competitor, a single use case).

When every page tries to speak to everyone, you end up speaking to no one. Segment by intent, then tighten the proof on that path only.

When to bring in outside messaging help

If your leadership team cannot finish the sentence “We are the right choice when ___” in under ten words, stop designing pages and fix that first. A workshop plus a tight messaging one-pager often saves weeks of visual iteration because the hierarchy writes itself.

We are happy to facilitate that conversation or work from a brief you already have—the goal is not poetry, it is a testable claim visitors can repeat back after thirty seconds on the site.
