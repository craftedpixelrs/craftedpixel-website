Timeline

What happens after your first email

Dates shift with scope, but the sequence doesn’t. Share this section with teammates who ask “when do we get a proposal?” before you book a call.

Proposals and SOWs are always in English—that’s how we work with US and EU teams day to day.

  • Good first email — Goal, timeline pressure, budget band (even rough), links, and who decides.
  • NDAs — We sign mutual NDAs when either side needs it; it never blocks an initial triage reply.
  • No work without SOW — We don’t start billable design or dev until scope and a deposit are agreed in writing.
  1. Day 0 — You reach out

    Contact form or email

    Tell us what you’re shipping, who the users are, and any hard dates. Attach a deck only if it’s already the source of truth—otherwise a short written brief is faster for everyone.

  2. Within 1 business day

    Human triage

    A senior lead acknowledges receipt, asks 1–3 clarifying questions if needed, and suggests either a 30-minute intro or an async “not a fit” with a polite reason. No ticket queues.

  3. Days 2–5

    Intro call (optional) + alignment

    We walk through goals, constraints, and risks. You leave with a plain-language recap: recommended approach, rough timeline, and whether a formal proposal makes sense.

  4. Typically 2–5 business days after we agree to propose

    Written proposal & SOW

    Scope, milestones, deliverables, price, payment schedule, and start window. If something is out of scope, we say so explicitly so procurement isn’t guessing.

  5. Kickoff

    Signed SOW + deposit + access

    We schedule kickoff, stand up shared tools (Slack/Linear/Figma as agreed), and begin the Listen → Plan phase from our homepage process section.

Cadence

When you’ll see the first design checkpoint

Most UI/UX sprint engagements target a first shareable design review within 8–12 business days of kickoff—once the SOW is signed, deposit is in, and we have access plus an agreed brief.

Audit-only or snapshot packages move faster; work with heavy legal, procurement, or multi-team review gates needs more calendar time. We put the target window in the SOW so “radio silence” isn’t the default anxiety.

Policy

If the first creative round misses what we agreed

Design is subjective; misaligned briefs shouldn’t be. If milestone one doesn’t match the success criteria written in the SOW (audience, constraints, examples we referenced), we revise that milestone once at no additional fee before the next billable round.

Changing goals mid-flight is a change order, not a free pass—fair to both sides. If we’re still off after that revision, we pause, realign in writing, and only then continue billable work.

Honest exit: If a fit call or early discovery shows we’re the wrong studio, we say so directly—we’d rather stop than burn your budget on polite theater.

Before kickoff: use our public kickoff brief checklist to line up goals, links, stakeholders, and success criteria—it mirrors what we ask for in early calls so nothing important waits until week three.

Pricing

What drives the quote (and where the packages live)

The tabs on the homepage are meant to give you a frame—starting points we keep honest against what we actually ship. Final price always follows written scope in the SOW.

See packages & comparison tables →

Surface area

Page count, flows, locales/languages, and whether we’re talking marketing site, app, or both. More surface area means more design, QA, and stakeholder coordination.

Integrations

CRM, payments, SSO, headless CMS, ERP, or custom APIs. Each integration carries risk and a test plan—we call it out explicitly in the SOW.

Design maturity

Greenfield brand and UI versus evolving an existing system. “Just ship what we have” costs less than codifying components and tokens—until rework shows up later.

Timeline pressure

Parallel sprints, night builds for a fixed date, or a tied marketing launch all increase coordination load. We’ll say early if a date isn’t realistic.

Compliance & a11y depth

WCAG level, VPAT needs, sector regs—we widen the test matrix and documentation, not just a “best effort” line in the footer.

Stakeholder shape

Large approval committees, legal review cycles, or vendor security questionnaires add calendar time—sometimes hourly for workshop-style status sessions.

Fit

When we’re probably not the right studio

Saying “yes” to everything is how calendars catch fire and clients get half-finished work. Here are the situations where we’ll politely decline—or point you to a partner who lives in that lane.

None of this is a value judgment on your business. It’s how we keep senior attention on engagements we can actually ship.

  • Price shopping without scope — If the only signal is “beat this hourly rate,” we can’t price risk honestly. Start with outcomes and constraints instead.
  • 24/7 on-call production support — We’re not a NOC. We plan launches, handoffs, and bounded post-launch windows—not pager duty for legacy stacks we didn’t build.
  • Staff aug with no product owner — If decisions stall in committee and tickets have no single accountable owner, velocity collapses. We do best with a clear PO or marketing lead.
  • Guaranteed rankings or viral lifts — We’ll improve structure, speed, and experiments—we won’t promise SERP #1 in 30 days or “viral” as a deliverable.
  • Unlimited liability or IP grabs — We carry normal professional coverage and fair IP assignment in the SOW. One-sided indemnities that wipe out a small studio are a hard no.
  • Impossible fixed dates + undefined scope — Tight launches are fine when trade-offs are explicit. “Everything in the deck, live next Friday” without flex needs a different team.
