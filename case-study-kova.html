Overview

Kova’s internal hiring product had grown faster than its UX. Recruiters were competent power users, but the interface rewarded memory over clarity—tabs multiplied, pipeline states competed for attention, and every new feature added visual noise.

We partnered with their product trio to redefine the core recruiter journey, ship a maintainable UI layer in React and TypeScript, and prove impact with hiring metrics—not satisfaction scores alone.

The constraint

Enterprise hiring is a network effect: one slow handoff ripples across dozens of open reqs. Kova’s team couldn’t afford a multi-quarter rewrite, but they also couldn’t keep bolting UI onto legacy patterns.

We mapped the top three failure moments in the funnel—requisition intake, candidate review, and scheduling handoffs—and agreed to optimize those first. Everything else waited in a ruthless backlog.

We didn’t need more dashboards. We needed fewer decisions per hire—and a system our engineers could extend without breaking the story.

— Aisha R., VP Product · Kova (composite quote; no photo/logo without client approval)

Approach

We ran two-week slices: prototype the riskiest flow, test with five recruiters, consolidate feedback once, then move to high-fidelity UI tied to tokens. Engineering joined mid-sprint for feasibility—not at the end as a surprise.

  • Journey maps for intake → offer, annotated with system states
  • Figma library: components, variants, and content guidelines
  • React implementation with typed props and Storybook previews
  • Staging QA against acceptance criteria tied to metrics

Design system in production

We treated the system as a product: semantic color roles, spacing rhythm, and elevation that worked in dark and light surfaces. Components shipped with empty, loading, and error states—not just the happy path.

The goal was obvious extension: a mid-level engineer should add a new pipeline column without inventing new CSS. Tokens and layout primitives did the guardrail work.

Results

After launch, recruiters spent less time reconciling tools and more time on candidate conversations. Time-to-hire improved 28% in Q1 versus the prior quarter baseline, with no change to headcount on the talent team.

We also saw fewer “workaround” spreadsheets—an informal signal that the product had become the source of truth again.
