Why a WordPress-only lane

WordPress rewards specialists: the block editor, plugin ecosystem, and hosting reality change fast. We treat it as a first-class product surface—not a theme swap or a page builder afterthought—whether you are launching a marketing site or running revenue on WooCommerce.

  • Greenfield & redesign — block themes, patterns, and editorial UX that match your brand system.
  • Custom engineering — plugins, REST/CLI, cron, and admin tools that belong in version control.
  • Care & evolution — tested updates, monitoring, and roadmap hours so the stack stays shippable.

01

Design, block themes & editorial UX

Full site editing when it fits; classic child themes when that’s the honest tradeoff—either way, editors get predictable layouts and components that match your brand.

We translate design into theme.json tokens, block styles, and patterns so marketing can ship without opening a ticket for every section. Locked templates, curated block allow-lists, and spacing presets keep quality high as more people touch the site.

When projects need structured data beyond blocks alone, we add ACF or custom post types deliberately—never as a default crutch—so the model stays understandable in five years.

Typical outputs

  • Block theme or child theme in Git
  • Pattern library + usage notes for editors
  • Accessibility pass on key templates
  • Performance budget & media strategy

Best when

You want WordPress to feel like a polished product—not a theme demo with your logo dropped in.

02

Custom plugins & APIs

Behavior that belongs in PHP gets a real plugin—namespaced, activatable, and reviewable—instead of anonymous snippets that rot in functions.php.

We build REST routes, admin list tables, settings pages, and WP-CLI commands when operators need leverage. Long-running work uses Action Scheduler or cron patterns that fail loudly and retry safely.

For headless or hybrid setups, we scope WPGraphQL, preview auth, and cache invalidation explicitly—the editorial experience and the frontend contract are designed together, not guessed after the fact.

Examples

  • Bespoke checkout or cart adjacency logic
  • CRM sync, webhooks, signed callbacks
  • Internal tools & role-specific dashboards
  • Migrators and one-off WP-CLI tasks
03

WooCommerce & memberships

Commerce flows are product work: gateways, subscriptions, tax, email, and fulfillment hooks—tested as a system, not as a pile of extensions.

We customize checkout, thank-you paths, and account areas with rollback plans. High-risk surfaces get a QA matrix (guest vs logged-in, coupon edge cases, failed payments) before anyone touches production.

HPOS-ready patterns, Action Scheduler hygiene, and observability for webhook failures keep operations understandable as order volume grows.

We stay honest about

Third-party gateway and tax rules vary by territory—we document assumptions, test with realistic data, and leave runbooks for your finance and support teams.

04

Integrations & data bridges

WordPress as the hub—or as a spoke—talking to CRMs, ESPs, analytics, DAMs, and custom backends with clear ownership of retries and idempotency.

We map server-to-server flows, queue work that shouldn’t block requests, and surface failures where your team already looks (admin notices, logs, or your monitoring stack).

Consent, PII minimization, and retention get a written checklist aligned with your legal reviewers—implementation discipline, not pretend compliance.

Common pairs

  • HubSpot / Salesforce / Pipedrive
  • Stripe / Adyen / local gateways
  • GA4 / GTM (with consent mode)
  • Algolia / custom search APIs
05

Migrations & rescue

Moving off Drupal, custom CMS, or a fragile WordPress install—URL maps, content ETL, media hygiene, and redirect testing so equity and revenue don’t quietly leak.

We start with inventory and risk: what must preserve URLs, what can be consolidated, and where editors need new workflows. Migrations run through staging with diffable scripts—not one-click importers nobody can replay.

Rescue work means stabilizing first: backups, update path, malware or bloat audit, then a sequenced plan so each merge leaves the site healthier.

You receive

  • Redirect tables + Search Console notes
  • Content validation reports
  • Rollback story per milestone
06

Hosting, ops & security

Staging that matches prod, backups someone verifies, and an update order that won’t white-screen checkout on a Friday.

We work with your host or recommend patterns: object caching, opcode cache, CDN rules, and database hygiene appropriate to traffic. Deploy paths might be Git-based, platform-native, or disciplined SFTP—documented either way.

Security means least-privilege roles, sensible file permissions, WAF/CDN notes, and a tested plugin/core update cadence. We don’t hide behind “WordPress is insecure”—we engineer for reality.

Practices

  • Environment parity checklist
  • Backup restore drill
  • Uptime + webhook monitoring hooks

  • Changelog for stakeholders

Roadmap slice

  • PRs or deploy packages you can audit
  • Office hours for “is this safe?” questions

  • Any compliance or change-window rules
