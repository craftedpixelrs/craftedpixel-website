3D & rendering

How we plug in

We treat 3D like any other design deliverable: agreed camera grammar, naming, resolution targets, and export presets—so your team isn’t guessing which file goes on the homepage versus paid social.

  • Model & light — hard-surface products, simplified hero scenes, studio or environmental sets.
  • Motion — turntables, short loops, and storyboarded beats for landing pages.
  • Web-ready — GLB/USDZ, poly budgets, and notes for Three.js or native viewers.

01

Still renders & packshots

Hero frames, catalog angles, and lifestyle composites—with print-safe resolution where you need it.

We block out shot lists against your grid: safe areas for headlines, vertical crops for social, and consistent camera height so a series reads as one brand.

When CAD exists we rebuild clean meshes for shading; when not, we model from references with agreed tolerances on detail level.

Typical outputs

  • Master PNG/TIFF + web derivatives
  • Named layers / passes where useful
  • Shot list + revision log
  • Color profile notes (sRGB / P3)

Best when

You are launching hardware, packaging, or a premium SaaS hero and stock photography feels off-brand.

02

Web-ready 3D

GLB/USDZ and poly budgets that your front-end team can drop into Three.js, Spline handoffs, or native AR quick looks.

We bake textures, limit draw calls, and document scale/orientation so the first integration isn’t a guessing game.

When you need configurators we align variant logic with how your CMS or PIM names SKUs—without over-promising real-time shaders we can’t maintain.

Typical outputs

  • GLB + optional USDZ
  • Poly/tex budget sheet
  • Pivot & scale convention
  • Integration notes for devs

Best when

Marketing wants a “spin the product” moment on the site without shipping a 40 MB model.

03

Motion & loops

Turntables, explainer beats, and social-length loops with safe margins for UI overlays and captions.

We storyboard in rough greyscale before lighting spend, and export codec presets for web (H.264/HEVC) plus ProRes when your editor needs headroom.

Length, frame rate, and loop points are agreed up front so paid teams aren’t recutting every asset.

Typical outputs

  • MP4/WebM + master MOV
  • Storyboard PDF
  • Loop-safe variants (9:16, 1:1)
  • SFX / music guidance (optional)

Best when

You need motion that matches the same product truth as your stills—not mismatched stock video.

04

Look dev & brand alignment

Materials and lighting that respect your palette, typography rhythm, and photography style—so 3D sits next to UI, not against it.

We pull tokens from your design system or brand guide and translate them into shader-friendly values—metallic/roughness, not mystery sliders.

When you already have a 2D illustration language, we discuss where 3D should echo it versus where it should stay photoreal.

Typical outputs

  • Look dev sheet (refs + targets)
  • Material library snapshot
  • Sign-off still before batch renders
  • Handoff to brand & systems when needed

Best when

Leadership cares that every touchpoint feels like one studio—not “the 3D vendor” off on their own island.

Cadence

What a typical two weeks looks like

A focused still or web-3D slice; larger motion passes may span additional sprints.

Week 1

  • Shot list + reference lock
  • Block mesh / scale check-in
  • Greyscale lighting pass

Week 2

  • Look dev sign-off still
  • Full-res renders or GLB v1
  • Package handoff + naming sheet

What we need from you

  • Brand guide or UI tokens
  • CAD, refs, or physical samples (photos)
  • Target resolutions & aspect ratios
  • Front-end owner if web GLB is in scope

You might start elsewhere

If the bottleneck is information architecture or performance on the live site, design and engineering often come before another render pass.

  • UI/UX — layout and narrative before hero art
  • Web development — ship the viewer and perf budget
  • Brand & systems — lock palette and photography rules first
