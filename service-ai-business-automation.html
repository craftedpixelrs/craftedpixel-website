How we plug in

We treat AI as one layer in a working system: first we stabilize how data moves and who owns each step, then we add models where they reduce touch time or error rate—with review gates, logs, and rollback you can defend in an audit.

  • Discovery — map reality, not the org chart; prioritize ROI.
  • Integrate — APIs, webhooks, and queues that don’t rot silently.
  • AI where it earns — extraction, routing, drafts—with human checkpoints.

01

Process & systems discovery

Shadow the real work: who touches a record, which SaaS owns truth, and where Slack becomes the database—before we automate the wrong slice.

We produce swimlanes, trigger inventories, and a ranked backlog: quick wins vs. structural fixes. AI opportunities are tagged only where a model can beat a rule today.

ROI framing is blunt—hours saved, error reduction, or speed to cash—so finance sees why the build order isn’t alphabetical.

Typical outputs

  • As-is / to-be workflow map
  • System-of-record matrix
  • Risk & compliance notes
  • Phased backlog with effort bands

Best when

Everyone describes the process differently, or last year’s “automation” is now three brittle Zaps nobody owns.

02

Integrations & data plumbing

REST, GraphQL, webhooks, queues, and file drops—designed for retries, idempotency, and observability when HubSpot sneezes at 2 a.m.

We document auth (OAuth, API keys, mutual TLS), rate limits, and backoff. Payloads are versioned so downstream automations don’t snap on field renames.

When you need a warehouse or lakehouse handoff, we align schemas with whoever owns BI—so ops automation and analytics don’t fork.

Typical outputs

  • Integration spec + sequence diagrams
  • Secret & env handling notes
  • Staging test data & fixtures
  • Runbook for common failures

Best when

CSV exports and copy-paste bridge two teams, or every integration is a one-off Node script on someone’s laptop.

03

Workflow automation

Make, n8n, Zapier, or lightweight workers in your cloud—branching logic, human approvals, and dead-letter paths operators can actually follow.

We bias to tools your team can maintain, with naming conventions and folder structure. Every flow ships with monitoring hooks—not “it worked on my machine.”

When volume or compliance demands code-first runners, we keep boundaries clear: what lives in vendor UI vs. repo, and how changes get reviewed.

Typical outputs

  • Exported scenario + version notes
  • Alert routing (Slack / email / Pager)
  • Operator checklist & RACI
  • Hypercare window plan

Best when

Tickets bounce between tools, or onboarding still needs four manual emails per customer.

04

AI layer: agents, RAG & extraction

Models grounded on approved corpora, structured outputs, and evaluation sets—plus human review for anything customer-facing or legally sensitive.

Use cases we ship: document classification, entity extraction, ticket summarization, lead routing hints, and internal Q&A over handbook + policy PDFs (RAG).

We document model choice, temperature caps, red-team prompts, and what happens when the API is down—fallback to rules, not silence.

Typical outputs

  • Prompt / tool spec with eval cases
  • Retrieval index & refresh policy
  • Human review queue design
  • Logging & cost monitoring notes

Best when

Teams drown in unstructured intake, or leadership wants “ChatGPT for our docs” without leaking confidential data.

05

Governance, safety & handoff

PII boundaries, retention, access control, and change management—so security and legal can sign off without blocking every iteration.

We map which fields never leave region, which automations require dual control, and how to roll back a bad model push. Vendor SLAs and subprocessors are summarized for your review packet.

Handoff includes training and a clear owner for dictionary updates—whether that’s ops, IT, or a shared guild.

Typical outputs

  • Data flow & PII diagram
  • Model / vendor risk summary
  • Incident & rollback playbook
  • Change-review checklist

Best when

You operate under SOC 2, HIPAA-adjacent, or EU data rules—or procurement asks for an actual architecture diagram.

Cadence

What a typical two weeks looks like

Typical slice for a first workflow or AI pilot—longer programs stack these cadences.

Week 1

  • Access to systems in scope (sandbox preferred)
  • Process workshop + draft swimlane
  • Integration spike on riskiest API

Week 2

  • Build v1 flow in staging / dev workspace
  • Monitoring + alert wiring
  • Operator walkthrough & failure drills

What we need from you

  • Named ops owner for approvals
  • API credentials or vendor admin
  • Sample payloads / tickets (sanitized)
  • Security reviewer contact (if required)

You might start elsewhere

If the real problem is front-end performance, design clarity, or editorial chaos, automation won’t fix the underlying bottleneck.

  • Web development — APIs, auth, and hosting for reliable workers
  • Content & CMS — structured data before AI over documents
  • Analytics & experimentation — measure before you optimize loops with AI
