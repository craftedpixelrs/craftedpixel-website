Overview

Čikinko needed a brand that could stretch from shelf to screen—distinct at small sizes, legible in motion, and disciplined enough for partners and retailers to reuse without breaking it.

We delivered a full branding program: strategic positioning hooks, logo system, color and typography, art direction notes for packaging and photography, plus Figma libraries and coded tokens so marketing and product share one language.

The constraint

Consumer brands ship across dozens of touchpoints before a designer touches them again—wholesale PDFs, Instagram stories, retailer co-marketing, and landing pages built by agencies you haven’t met yet.

We optimized for governance without bureaucracy: clear tiers of logo use, minimum sizes, color accessibility checks, and component rules that prevent “accidental new fonts.”

We wanted a system bold enough for packaging, calm enough for product UI, and documented enough that partners stop improvising.

— Client stakeholder · Čikinko (paraphrased; pending final approval)

Approach

Start with narrative territory and proof points, then parallelize identity + digital UI kit so neither waits on the other. Reviews are milestone-based with exportable assets each round.

  • Logo system: primary, stacked, icon mark, clear-space rules
  • Color roles, gradients (if any), and accessible pairings for web
  • Typography scale for marketing vs. product UI
  • Figma UI kit + handoff notes for dev (tokens, components)

Design system in production

Components are grouped by job: marketing hero, editorial, product chrome, and social—each references the same tokens so campaigns and the app feel related.

We document do / don’t patterns for photography, illustration, and iconography so future packs stay on-brand.

Results

Brand programs measure speed and consistency as much as clicks: faster packaging rounds, fewer one-off agency fees for “just resize this,” and cleaner approvals because rules are explicit.

Drop your quantitative lifts here post-launch (awareness, retailer feedback, site engagement)—we left room in the layout for charts when you have them.
