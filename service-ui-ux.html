01

Product & UX strategy

We frame the problem before pixels—so design choices trace back to user and business goals, not the loudest opinion in the room.

We start by mapping who you serve, what “success” means in measurable terms, and which journeys matter most. That becomes a shared brief: problem statements, opportunity areas, and guardrails (accessibility, brand, tech) that every later artifact respects.

For greenfield products we lean on lightweight workshops and competitive scans. For mature apps we add heuristic reviews and analytics hints so we don’t redesign what already works.

Typical outputs

  • Problem / outcome brief & success metrics
  • User journeys & prioritized scenarios
  • IA proposals & navigation models
  • Workshop notes & decision log

Best when

Stakeholders disagree on scope, you’re entering a new market, or engineering is waiting on a single source of truth before they estimate.

02

Wireframes & prototyping

From gray boxes to clickable flows—so you can test assumptions early and give engineering something concrete to size.

We move from low-fidelity structure to high-fidelity interaction in deliberate steps. Every screen has named states: default, loading, empty, error, success—so nobody discovers edge cases on launch week.

Prototypes are built for the audience: stakeholder walkthroughs, usability tests, or dev reference. We keep components tied to your design system when one exists, or establish sensible defaults when you’re still forming one.

Typical outputs

  • Low-fi wireframes & flow maps
  • Clickable prototype (Figma / FigJam)
  • Annotated user flows & state diagrams
  • Test scripts when research is in scope

Best when

You need sign-off before build, you’re validating a new funnel, or PMs want a single artifact for estimation.

03

Visual & UI design

Typography, color, density, and motion that feel intentional—aligned with your brand and resilient across breakpoints.

We design UI that survives real content: long labels, translated strings, and dense data. Components are spec’d with spacing, hover, focus, and disabled states so accessibility isn’t an afterthought.

When you already have a system, we extend it. When you don’t, we ship a coherent kit you can grow—without a thousand one-off styles.

Typical outputs

  • High-fidelity UI for agreed scope
  • Responsive layouts & breakpoint notes
  • Component specs & variant matrix
  • Iconography & illustration direction

Best when

Marketing and product look like two different companies, or engineering is blocked waiting for final pixels.

04

User research & testing

Enough evidence to de-risk decisions—without turning every release into a six-month study.

We run moderated or unmoderated tests against prototypes or live builds, recruit the right audience (or work with your customers), and synthesize findings into prioritized fixes—not a slide deck nobody opens.

When budgets are tight, we combine targeted interviews with heuristic passes and analytics breadcrumbs so you still move forward with eyes open.

Typical outputs

  • Research plan & discussion guide
  • Session recordings & highlights reel
  • Findings report with severity & effort
  • Backlog-ready recommendations

Best when

Leadership wants proof before budget, conversion dropped after a change, or you’re betting on a new audience.

05

Design QA & dev handoff

What ships matches what we signed off on—tokens, spacing, and behavior documented the way engineers actually consume them.

We export structured handoffs: naming that maps to code, redlines where they help, and links to dev mode. During build we do spot QA on staging—catching regressions before your users do.

If you use Storybook or a design-system pipeline, we align variables and component props so design and code share one vocabulary.

Typical outputs

  • Dev-ready Figma files & version history
  • Token / variable export notes
  • Staging review checklist & bug log
  • Launch sign-off with design + eng

Best when

Past launches drifted from mockups, or you’re scaling a team and need repeatable handoff hygiene.

Cadence

What a typical two weeks looks like

Every engagement flexes—but this rhythm keeps stakeholders aligned without endless status theater.

Week 1

  • Kickoff + access to analytics, repo links, and brand files
  • Workshop or async brief to lock problem & success metrics
  • Low-fi flows for the riskiest journey; quick internal review

Week 2

  • Clickable prototype or hi-fi slice for the agreed scope
  • Stakeholder walkthrough + consolidated feedback log
  • Handoff package or research plan for the next increment

What we need from you

  • A single product owner (or triad) who can decide
  • Read-only Figma / FigJam or equivalent for async reviews
  • An engineer in the loop for feasibility checks mid-sprint
  • Brand guidelines or permission to define interim tokens

You might start elsewhere

UI/UX is the right door when discovery, interaction, or visual clarity is the bottleneck. If the real constraint is implementation, SEO, or raw brand story, another engagement usually ships value faster.

  • Web development — production, refactors, and performance
  • Brand & design systems — narrative, identity, and tokens before UI volume
  • SEO & growth — when traffic and conversion science come first
